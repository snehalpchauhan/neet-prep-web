Preparation of Haloarenes
~7 min Reactions Classic NEET Qs
🏭
Chlorobenzene was used to make DDT — but benzene resists halogen addition, so you need a Lewis acid catalyst to force the reaction!
Method 1 — Direct EAS halogenation
C₆H₆ + Cl₂ → C₆H₅Cl
Catalyst: FeCl₃

C₆H₆ + Br₂ → C₆H₅Br
Catalyst: FeBr₃
This is electrophilic substitution, not addition. The Lewis acid activates X₂ → makes X⁺ electrophile. Direct F₂ is too violent; direct I₂ is too slow — both need special methods.

Elimination Reactions: E1 & E2
~7 min Mechanism Saytzeff's rule tested
🚗
When traffic is too congested, cars take an exit ramp — when a carbon gets too crowded, molecules eliminate to form a double bond and take a different route!
E1 vs E2
E1 — Unimolecular
Rate = k[RX]
① Carbocation forms (SLOW)
② Base removes β-H (FAST)

Needs: weak base, polar protic solvent, 3° halide, high temp
E2 — Bimolecular
Rate = k[RX][Base]
Base removes β-H & X⁻ leaves simultaneously

Needs: strong bulky base (KOH/alc, KOtBu), anti-periplanar geometry
☑ Saytzeff's Rule — the major product is always…
More substituted alkene
✓
More stable = major product
vs
Less substituted alkene
✗
Less stable = minor product
Alcoholic KOH
→ Elimination (E1/E2)
Aqueous KOH
→ Substitution (SN)
NEET TIP
Alcoholic KOH → elimination is the most frequently tested condition in this atom. The condition tells you the product.
✕ Less substituted = Saytzeff product (it's more substituted) ✕ Aqueous KOH gives elimination — aqueous = substitution, alcoholic = elimination

Reactions of Haloarenes
~7 min Reactions Tricky NEET Qs
🏛️
Benzene is like a VIP lounge — nucleophiles are usually turned away at the door! But with the right EWG pass, even they can enter.
NAS — why haloarenes resist nucleophiles
3 reasons ArX resists NAS
① C–X has partial double bond (resonance) → harder to break
② sp² C less electrophilic than sp³
③ Steric hindrance from ring
When NAS is possible
• Extreme conditions: 300°C, 300 atm
• OR –NO₂ groups ortho/para to X

Mechanism: addition-elimination via Meisenheimer complex
EAS — halogen's dual role
+M effect (resonance)
o/p director
Lone pair donates to ring via resonance → directs incoming electrophile to ortho and para positions
–I effect (induction)
Ring deactivator
Withdraws electrons inductively → ring has less electron density → EAS slower than benzene
Net result: Halogens are o/p directors but ring deactivators — EAS gives o + p products, but slower than benzene
NEET TIP
The most tricky question: halogens are o/p directors — students assume they activate the ring. They DON'T. o/p director ≠ ring activator for halogens.
✕ o/p directors always activate ring — halogens direct o/p but DEactivate ✕ Haloarenes can't do NAS — they can, with EWG groups or extreme conditions
